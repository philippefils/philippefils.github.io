EMBEDDED SYSTEMS / PCB DESIGN

Custom ESP32-S3 MP3 Player

Custom embedded audio player designed around an ESP32-S3, rechargeable LiPo power system, microSD storage, and a dedicated headphone audio chain.

Embedded Systems PCB Design Audio Power Electronics

OVERVIEW

Custom Portable Audio Platform

This project is a custom MP3 player built around the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8. The system is designed to play WAV audio files stored on a microSD card, provide headphone output through a dedicated audio signal chain, and operate from a rechargeable single-cell LiPo battery.

The board integrates USB-C connectivity for charging and data transfer, battery monitoring, a character display, physical controls, a volume potentiometer, and dedicated audio and power circuitry into a single custom PCB.

My work focused on the electrical hardware design, including schematic capture, component selection, power architecture, audio signal-chain design, PCB layout, interface design, assembly, and hardware bring-up.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Embedded Audio Architecture

The ESP32-S3 acts as the central controller, coordinating audio playback, user input, display control, storage, and power-related functions. Audio files are read from the microSD card and streamed through the ESP32-S3's I²S interface to the external DAC.

The resulting analog audio signal is passed from the DAC to the headphone amplifier before reaching the 3.5 mm audio jack. The digital and analog portions of the design were treated as separate functional areas to reduce unwanted coupling into the audio path.

AUDIO SYSTEM

Digital-to-Analog Audio Chain

Audio playback begins with WAV files stored on the microSD card. The ESP32-S3 reads the audio data over SPI and sends the digital audio stream to an external DAC through I²S.

The DAC converts the digital audio into an analog signal, which is then passed through a dedicated headphone amplifier before being routed to the 3.5 mm audio output.

The audio section also includes dedicated analog power filtering and local decoupling to limit digital switching noise from coupling into the sensitive analog circuitry.

POWER DESIGN

Rechargeable Battery Power System

The power system is built around a single-cell LiPo battery and USB-C input. The charging stage provides the battery charging function while supporting system power during operation.

A buck-boost regulator generates the main 3.3 V rail used by the ESP32-S3 and other low-voltage circuitry. A separate filtered analog supply is used for the audio circuitry to reduce the impact of digital switching noise.

Battery monitoring is also integrated into the design so that the remaining battery level can be measured by the ESP32-S3.

USER INTERFACE

Physical Controls and Display

The player uses physical buttons for playback and navigation, along with a dedicated character display for presenting playback and system information.

A Bourns 3352T-1-103LF thumbwheel potentiometer provides hardware volume control. The potentiometer is read by the ESP32-S3 ADC, with software smoothing applied to reduce small variations in the measured value.

PCB DESIGN

Four-Layer PCB Layout

The PCB was designed in Altium Designer as a four-layer board, allowing the digital, analog, power, and ground regions to be organized while maintaining controlled return paths.

The ESP32-S3, microSD interface, USB-C circuitry, display, buttons, and power electronics were placed to keep high-speed digital and switching-current paths away from the sensitive analog audio circuitry.

The audio DAC and headphone amplifier were grouped within the analog section of the board, with dedicated power filtering and local decoupling placed close to the relevant devices.

HARDWARE VALIDATION

Bring-Up and Hardware Testing

Hardware bring-up was performed progressively, beginning with power and charging circuitry before integrating the ESP32-S3 and peripheral interfaces.

Individual power rails were checked for expected voltage levels and shorts before connecting downstream circuitry. Peripheral interfaces and control signals were then verified during system integration.

The audio path was tested independently to verify signal transfer through the DAC and headphone amplifier before full system integration.

FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Future Development

Future revisions could further refine the audio power distribution and grounding strategy to reduce noise coupling between the digital and analog sections.

Additional hardware and firmware features could also expand the player with improved playback controls, additional audio formats, and more advanced battery and system monitoring.

The modular interface approach also provides opportunities to replace or expand individual peripherals without redesigning the entire system.
