AUTOMOTIVE HARDWARE

Formula Electric Shutdown Loop

Diagnostic PCB designed to monitor the state of the Formula Electric vehicle shutdown loop and simplify electrical fault identification.

Digital Logic MOSFET PCB Design Diagnostics

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Diagnostic hardware for vehicle safety systems

As part of the McGill Formula Electric team, I designed a diagnostic PCB for monitoring the state of the vehicle's shutdown loop. The shutdown loop is responsible for ensuring that critical vehicle systems are functioning before the high-voltage system can remain enabled.

The vehicle contains 13 critical systems, each providing a signal that contributes to the shutdown loop. If one of these systems fails, its signal changes state and prevents the vehicle from powering on or causes the shutdown loop to open while the vehicle is operating.

The purpose of the board was to make these faults immediately visible to the electrical team, significantly reducing the time required to identify which subsystem was responsible for a shutdown condition.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

13-channel shutdown loop monitoring

Each of the 13 monitored systems provides a shutdown-loop signal to the diagnostic board. Each channel independently monitors its corresponding signal and provides a visual indication when a fault occurs.

FAULT DETECTION

MOSFET-based fault indication

Each diagnostic channel uses a PMOS transistor to detect the corresponding shutdown-loop fault condition. During normal operation, the indicator remains off. When the monitored signal goes low because of a system fault, the PMOS transistor switches on and drives the corresponding LED.

This creates a direct visual relationship between a shutdown condition and the vehicle subsystem responsible for it. Instead of manually tracing the shutdown loop to determine which system caused the fault, the diagnostic board identifies the affected channel through its LED indicator.

PCB DESIGN

From schematic to diagnostic hardware

The circuit was developed from the shutdown-loop requirements into an electrical schematic and PCB layout using Altium Designer. The board integrates the 13 independent monitoring channels into a single compact diagnostic interface.

The design emphasizes clear channel identification and simple troubleshooting. Each LED corresponds directly to one monitored vehicle system, allowing the electrical team to quickly identify the source of a shutdown condition.

HARDWARE VALIDATION

Circuit verification and troubleshooting

The individual monitoring channels were verified by applying the expected shutdown-loop signal states and observing the corresponding LED response.

Continuity and electrical checks were used to verify the PCB connections and MOSFET circuitry before integration with the vehicle's electrical system.

The resulting diagnostic interface provided a straightforward method of identifying which monitored subsystem was responsible for a shutdown-loop fault.

FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Future improvements

A future revision could further improve the diagnostic interface by adding test points or additional measurement capability for each monitored shutdown-loop channel.

The board could also be expanded to provide additional diagnostic information beyond a simple fault indication, allowing the electrical team to distinguish between different failure modes during vehicle troubleshooting.
