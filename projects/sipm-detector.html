01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project Overview

In collaboration with the Montreal Neurological Institute, I developed a two-channel scintillation detector for monitoring radioactivity during the automated synthesis of positron emission tomography (PET) radiopharmaceuticals.

The system uses silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) to convert scintillation events into electrical pulses. The signals are amplified, peak-detected, and sampled by an ESP32 before being transmitted to a PC-based interface.

The objective was to develop a cost-effective, modular, and scalable detector suitable for integration into an automated radiopharmaceutical synthesis system.

03 — HARDWARE DESIGN

Analog Front-End

The SiPM produces relatively small electrical pulses that require amplification before being sampled by the microcontroller.

I designed the amplification stage around an OPA354 in a non-inverting configuration. The SiPM output is connected to the non-inverting input through a series resistor, while the feedback network controls the amplifier response.

Peak Detection

The amplified signal is processed by a peak-detection circuit using two OPA354 amplifiers, an NMOS transistor, and a BAT54S Schottky diode.

The circuit captures the peak of the detector pulse and converts the short-duration signal into a voltage that can be sampled by the ESP32 ADC.

04 — POWER ARCHITECTURE

Power Management

The board is powered from a 5 V barrel-jack input. A boost converter generates the approximately 29.5 V bias required by the SiPM.

The low-voltage circuitry operates from the 3.3 V supply associated with the ESP32. Local decoupling capacitors were placed throughout the design to support stable operation of the active devices.

05 — PCB DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION

PCB Development

I designed the complete two-layer PCB in Altium Designer, with the analog circuitry physically clustered together and sensitive signal paths kept as short and direct as practical.

The PCB was manufactured by JLCPCB and assembled by me using a combination of reflow soldering and hand soldering.

07 — TESTING & VALIDATION

Hardware Bring-Up & Testing

The analog stages were tested independently before integration into the complete detector.

A function generator was used to provide representative input signals while an oscilloscope was used to observe the response at different stages of the analog signal chain.

The completed detector was subsequently tested using the SiPM to capture actual detector pulses. A multimeter, oscilloscope, and function generator were used throughout hardware bring-up and debugging.

08 — ENGINEERING TAKEAWAYS

What I Would Improve

The project provided practical experience designing and integrating a complete mixed-signal measurement system, while also highlighting areas for improvement in a future revision.

Additional filtering of the analog signal path would be considered to improve noise rejection and signal conditioning.

The grounding and analog/digital power architecture could also be refined further. The prototype used a common ground architecture, providing an opportunity for a future revision to explore improved grounding and power distribution strategies.
